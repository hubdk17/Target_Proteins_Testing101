## 1. What the folder actually is

It is **not** a binder-design folder. It is a molecular-dynamics platform built around
**barnase–barstar (PDB 1BRS, chains C + F)** under Amber ff14SB / TIP3P-FB, that turned into
something more useful than a case study:

> a test rig for whether the descriptors computational structural biology relies on survive
> varying something they should be invariant to.

Six systems share one MD protocol (1BRS barnase–barstar, 4HFZ p53–MDM2, 4ZQK PD-1/PD-L1,
3ECU apo SOD1 dimer, 2J0Z p53 tetramer, 2PTC trypsin–BPTI), **446 ns** of production MD,
~367 GB. Only the 1BRS root is on this folder; the other five are sibling folders on the same
desktop that I cannot see from here (see §5).

The output is mostly **negative and bounding results**, and the folder is unusually honest about
them: `PLATFORM_REVIEW` §4 is a table of fifteen retracted or never-to-be-quoted claims,
including several of its own author's predictions. That section is the most valuable thing in it.

| Higher-order H-bond structure | z = 5.18 (1BRS), 10.11 (4ZQK) | **5.178 / 10.11 / 0.177** | same |

Controls that also held: Asp35 recovered 1.000, Glu46 0.667, Glu76 0.729 — the gradient the
burial mechanism predicts. Basic-residue suppression at the interface 0.0410 vs 0.1328 = 3.24×.

**One nuance, not an error.** The docs say "45 of 48 fitted bonds have a decreasing hazard."
Counting strictly on `k < 1` I get **47 of 48**. The difference is that the stored
`hazard_verdict` uses a band (≈0.9–1.1 counted as "memoryless") rather than a hard cut at 1.
The claim is robust either way.

**One invariant I checked before trusting it.** In `output/chirality_audit.json`, the three
1BRS seeds report *identical* median and minimum signed volumes to full precision — the kind of
across-sample invariant this project treats as a bug until proved otherwise. Here it is correct:
the audit reads each run's `topology_protein.pdb`, and all three seeds share one equilibrated
starting topology; seeds differ in velocities, not atoms. Same for the three 4ZQK seeds.
The consequence is worth stating though: **the audit gates the built structure, not the
trajectory.** It would not catch an inversion that happened during production. It does catch the
known mechanism, since the topology is written after minimisation and equilibration.

## 3. Four things in here that should change what the binder pipeline does

Ordered by how much they change what to do on Monday.

### 3.1 Your PD-1 target structure has five D-amino acids in it

`25_chirality_audit.py` found, **identically in all three seeds, deterministically**:

```
4ZQK PD-1 chain B:  Asp85  Ser87  Gln88  Pro89  Gln91     signed volume NEGATIVE
```

Signed volume of (N, C, CB) about CA is positive for L-amino acids (median here +0.0025 nm³);
these five read −0.0016 to −0.0028 nm³. They are five of the eight residues of the **85–92 loop,
which is absent from the 4ZQK crystal and was built de novo by PDBFixer's `addMissingAtoms()`**.

Why nothing would have told you: **Amber protein force fields carry no improper term on the Cα
centre.** Chirality is held by starting geometry alone — nothing restrains it, nothing restores
it. Energy, bond lengths, RMSD, radius of gyration, DSSP, contact occupancy and interface contact
counts all read completely normal on a D-residue.

Two consequences for us:

- **PD-1 is first in the multi-target batch, and 4ZQK is the structure.** Of 17 PD-1 interface
  residues exactly one is affected — **Asp85, 0.286 nm from PD-L1** — so 16 of 17 are
  crystallographic and most of the system is fine. But any hotspot, contig boundary or contact
  reading that touches **85–92 is resting on invented, stereochemically wrong geometry.** This is
  worth checking directly against whatever we settle the PD-1 hotspots to be.
- **The general rule transfers to every stage of our pipeline.** Anything that rebuilds atoms on
  a de novo backbone is exposed. Separately measured in the same audit: OpenMM's
  `Modeller.addHydrogens` placed HA on the wrong side of the chiral centre in **one build in
  five**, nondeterministically, and minimisation then resolved the impossible carbon by inverting
  CB into a permanent D-residue. RFdiffusion emits backbones, MPNN emits sequence, and the
  complex gets assembled and hydrogenated downstream — that is exactly the exposed path, and
  neither gate 1 (Rg), gate 2 (backbone RMSD) nor gate 3 (pae_interaction) can see it.

**The gate is cheap.** Signed volume of (N, C, CB) about CA, one sign test per residue. Note
their hard-won detail: use the **signed volume, not the N-CA-C-CB improper dihedral** — the
improper swings continuously and gave misleading readings during the diagnosis. And a position
restraint is not sufficient (CB moved 0.1868 nm for ~35 kJ/mol).

### 3.2 MM-GBSA cannot be our missing interaction-energy stage — unless charges are complementary

This folder is the only place in the whole project where **interaction energy between chains is
actually computed**, which is precisely the hole in our pipeline. The result is a hard boundary:

| | net charges | ΔE_inter (vac) | ΔG_solv (GB) | ΔG_eff | cancellation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| barnase–barstar | +2 / −6 **complementary** | −668.8 | +574.0 | **−94.8** | 6.7× |
| trypsin–BPTI | +6 / +6 **like** | **+178.99** | **−176.02** | **+2.98** | **60.8×** |

Trypsin–BPTI has Kd ~10⁻¹⁴ M and an experimental ΔG_bind near −19 kcal/mol. The method returns
**positive**. And its CV is 5.6% against barnase's 6.26% — **equally reproducible on both
systems, correct on only one.** It returns a precisely wrong answer, not a noisy one.

For us this is directly actionable and slightly alarming: MPNN suppresses basic residues at
designed interfaces by 3.2× (§3.3), so **our designed binders will tend to come out net
negative.** If the target is also net negative, any implicit-solvent ΔG ranking we bolt on sits
in the 60× cancellation regime and the sign is essentially random — silently. Before trusting
any such score, compute both net charges and refuse on like signs. A `charge_complementarity_gate()`
exists in `24_mmgbsa_ensemble.py`, **written, documented, and never called from `main()`**.

Their conclusion, which I think is right: do not try MM-PBSA, the cancellation is structural. The
rigorous route is alchemical ΔΔG. That matches what our own notes already say about MD being the
far-end positive control, weeks away.

### 3.3 What ProteinMPNN reads from the target, and what it does not

Measured on 288 designs (3 arms × 3 seeds × 2 temperatures × 16), model `v_48_020`:

- **It fails on buried charge.** Barstar **Asp39** — the single most important residue at the
  interface, partner to four of the five salt bridges — has acidic fraction **0.000 in 96/96**
  target-aware designs. Mechanism is burial, measured: Asp39's SASA is **0.0078 nm²** in the
  complex against 0.9913 alone, the most occluded interface residue. Asp35, exposed, is recovered
  100%.
- **The target-dependent signal is negative design, not positive design.** Basic frequency at the
  interface is suppressed 3.2× (0.0410 vs 0.1328) while **acidic frequency does not rise at all**
  (Δ −0.018, inverted). Measuring only "acidic frequency" would have shown nothing.
- **Trypsin Asp189 is the same motif independently**: SASA 0.013 nm² in the complex, and it is
  the specificity keystone. Two systems say the residue hardest for the tool to recover is the
  one that matters most.

This sharpens our own stage-2 finding rather than contradicting it. Our audit showed MPNN
*does* read target chemistry strongly — one surface mutation on IL-6 rewrote 8–11 of 40–55
positions. This folder says **what** it reads: it reads the target well enough to avoid placing a
clashing charge, and not well enough to place a buried one. For IL-6 site III, if a productive
binder needs a buried charge against the target face, stage 2 will not propose it, and stage 2 is
where the sequence is committed.

Honest limit they state and I agree with: removing the target also unburies the interface, so
target-driven and burial-driven effects are **inseparable** without a shape-matched decoy.

### 3.4 A contact-count screen can miss a change that destroys the interaction

Three independent instances in this platform. The sharpest, from trypsin–BPTI:

**D189A deletes the S1 specificity determinant.** The P1 lysine moved **0.43 nm** (pocket floor
to pocket mouth, ~44× the combined noise) and swapped its **entire** coordination shell:

| Lys15 NZ partner | WT | D189A |
|---|---|---|
| Ser190 O | 0.999 | **0.027** |
| Ser190 OG | 0.997 | **0.023** |
| Trp215 O | 0.512 | **0.008** |
| Gly219 O | 0.012 | **0.989** |
| Gly216 O | 0.008 | **0.939** |

Total interface contacts over the same window: **283.2 → 282.6**, against a noise floor of 2.84.
**A contact-count screen would have scored this mutation as having no effect whatsoever.**

Also: an isosteric neutral acceptor (D189N) held ~15 ns then collapsed to the deletion's end
state, so **charge, not H-bond geometry, is the determinant**; and a charge-matched distal control
(E186A, 1.66 nm away, same net charge and ion count) is indistinguishable from WT, so the effect
is the site and not the charge change.

This is the same lesson our W157A audit learned the hard way — global Rg saw nothing (23% vs 24%)
while interface-local metrics saw t ≈ 6.2 — now confirmed on a different system with a different
observable. It is a reason to keep the complement control (keep the name TRP, delete the side
chain) framed in interface-local terms, and a reason not to add any contact-count tiebreaker to
gate 1.

## 4. Three things I found that the folder's own write-ups do not say

### 4.1 The GB model choice moves ΔG_eff by 26 kcal/mol, and both options are "reproducible"

`mmgbsa_ensemble.json` stores **two** implicit-solvent models. The docs quote only the first:

| GB model | per-seed ΔG_eff (42 / 137 / 256) | mean | cross-seed CV |
|---|---|---|---|
| `implicit/obc2.xml` | −95.01 / −110.21 / −106.76 | **−103.995** | 6.26% |
| `implicit/gbn2.xml` | −72.50 / −81.64 / −80.11 | **−78.081** | 5.12% |

The two standard models disagree by **25.9 kcal/mol — 4× the cross-seed SD (6.5) and 25% of the
value** — while each reproduces across seeds at CV 5–6%. This is a second, independent instance
of the platform's own central thesis, sitting inside the same file: reproducibility is necessary
and not sufficient. It also means the charge-complementarity gate, while correct, is not the only
ungated degree of freedom in that number. **Any ΔG_eff quoted from here should name its GB model.**

### 4.2 Twenty times the frames bought zero independent information

Same file. Seed 137 is one trajectory stored at both rates, so this is a clean held-fixed test:

| | frames | n_eff (obc2) |
|---|---|---|
| seed 137, 0.5 ps | 40,000 | **35.86** |
| seed 137, subsampled to 10 ps | 2,000 | **35.95** |

**20× the frames, identical effective sample size.** And across two identical-protocol seeds,
n_eff runs 35.9 (seed 137) to 96.7 (seed 256) — a 2.7× spread, so the per-seed SEMs are not
comparable to one another and the 3-seed CV rests on ~36–97 independent samples, not 40,000
frames. This is the platform's frame-rate divide showing up in the energetics, where it had not
been pointed out.

### 4.3 MPNN charge-reverses a salt-bridge partner in one design in five — with the target present

Barstar **Glu76** is the partner of barnase **Arg59**. Across the 96 target-aware designs:

```
Asp 37   Glu 33   |   Lys 19   Arg 1   |   Asn 1   Gln 1   Ser 4
```

The docs report "Glu76 73% acidic." The part not drawn out is that the non-acidic fraction is
mostly **basic**: **20 of 96 designs (20.8%) place a positive residue directly opposite the
target's arginine.** With the target structure supplied. That is the negative-design mechanism of
§3.3 failing at the one salt bridge the platform classifies as "edge" rather than "core".

For our pipeline this is the concrete version of the core claim: a like-charge clash at a
designed interface is a real failure mode at ~20% per position, and **nothing in our gate stack
can see it.** Gate 2 is a fold check. Gate 3's pae_interaction is a confidence metric — AF2 will
happily be confident about a geometrically plausible, electrostatically wrong interface. A
per-position charge-compatibility check against the target face is cheap and would catch it.

## 6. One more thing, applied to our own data

This folder's §2.11 result is that **noise floors computed at the wrong sample size manufacture
findings** — a p53 design was flagged at 4.8% as real until the length-matched floor turned out
to be 7.05%. Applying the same discipline to our six-target gate-1 pass rates (50 designs each):

| target | pass | k/50 | 95% CI (Wilson) |
|---|---|---|---|
| pcsk9 | 40% | 20/50 | 27.6 – 53.8% |
| glp1r | 38% | 19/50 | 25.9 – 51.8% |
| pd1 | 20% | 10/50 | 11.2 – 33.0% |
| bcl2 | 6% | 3/50 | 2.1 – 16.2% |
| casp3 | 6% | 3/50 | 2.1 – 16.2% |
| brca1 | 0% | 0/50 | 0.0 – 7.1% |

Adjacent comparisons: pcsk9 vs glp1r **z = +0.21**, bcl2 vs casp3 **z = 0.00**, casp3 vs brca1
**z = +1.76**, glp1r vs pd1 **z = +1.98**, pd1 vs bcl2 **z = +2.08**.

**At 50 designs each, the ladder resolves about three tiers and no finer:** {pcsk9, glp1r} ≈ 40%,
{pd1} ≈ 20%, {bcl2, casp3, brca1} ≤ 6%. The ordering *within* those groups is noise — pcsk9
above glp1r, and casp3 above brca1, are not differences. Even the tier boundaries are only ~2σ
uncorrected, which across five adjacent comparisons is not significance. The three-tier
structure is sound; any statement that ranks pcsk9 over glp1r is not.

This does not change the plan. It changes what we may say about it, and it means the 5-hotspot
rerun should be read as "does the tier move", not "did the number go up".

## 7. Recommendation

One thing, cheap, and nothing else in our stack can do it:

**Add a signed-volume chirality gate to the complex-building step**, and run it once over the
existing pool and over the PD-1 inputs. One sign test per residue on (N, C, CB) about CA; L is
positive. It costs seconds, it catches a defect that is invisible to Rg, backbone RMSD,
pae_interaction and every contact metric we have, and the PD-1 structure we are about to design
against is a measured, deterministic instance of the failure.

Second, if you want an interaction-energy stage at all: **wire the charge-complementarity gate
before anything else**, because the alternative is a score that is confidently wrong rather than
noisy, and our own designs are biased toward the net charge that breaks it.
