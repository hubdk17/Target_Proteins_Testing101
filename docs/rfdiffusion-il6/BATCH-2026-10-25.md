Eight Boltz conditions, 100 diffusion samples each, queued to run unattended
over a two-day absence. This file is the key for reading the results: without
it, `boltz_batch_out/` is eight directories of numbers with no design.

Project framing: `README.md`. First scoring run and the noise floor that
motivated 100 samples: `STAGE3-boltz-scoring.md`. Commands: `METHODS.md`.

## The eight conditions

| file | chain A | chain B | what it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wt.yaml` | `A` | `B` | the measurement, at proper sampling |
| `w157a.yaml` | `AW` | `B` | **the project's question** |
| `e93a.yaml` | `AE` | `B` | non-interface mutation control |
| `k120a.yaml` | `AK` | `B` | second non-interface mutation control |
| `alaif.yaml` | `A` | `BA` | **go/no-go gate**: a deliberately destroyed interface |
| `random40.yaml` | `A` | `R` | negative control, gives the ipTM number a scale |
| `targetonly.yaml` | `A` | — | isolates the target-folding instability |
| `binderonly.yaml` | `B` | — | does `short_9` fold without IL-6 to lean on? |

### Why two non-interface controls rather than one

A control mutation has to be clear of the binder's footprint, and the
binder-side interface list does not say which *target* residues are contacted.
Both E93 and K120 are charged, so almost certainly surface, and both sit on
helices B and C rather than the helix A / helix D end where site III lies. That
is reasoning, not measurement. Two of them makes it unlikely that both are
accidentally at the interface. **On return, run the site III contact counter
from `METHODS.md` on the existing complex to check whether 93 or 120 appears in
the contacted set, and discard either one that does.**

### What was deliberately left out

The unrelated-target arm, which is gate level 1 in the plan. It needs a
sequence for some other protein, and nothing here can verify such a sequence
against coordinates the way the IL-6 string was verified. `alaif` and
`random40` already test the same proposition — does the metric respond when
there is no reason for binding — with sequences that *are* verified. The
unrelated-target arm is a three-minute job to add later.

## Why 100 samples

The first run gave `iptm` 0.581 with SD 0.116 at n = 5, so SEM 0.052. A hotspot
tryptophan to alanine would plausibly move ipTM by 0.05 to 0.15, which is inside
that. At 3.4 s per sample, n = 100 brings SEM to about 0.012, so an effect of
0.05 becomes resolvable at roughly four standard errors. The whole batch is
about an hour of A100, submitted with eight hours of headroom.

## How to read the results

```bash
cd ~/rfd
for d in boltz_batch_out/*/predictions/*/; do echo "--- $d"; grep -h '"iptm"' $d/confidence_*.json | tr -d ' ,"' | cut -d: -f2 | awk '{s+=$1;q+=$1*$1;n++} END{m=s/n;printf "n=%d mean=%.3f sd=%.3f sem=%.3f\n",n,m,sqrt((q-n*m*m)/(n-1)),sqrt((q-n*m*m)/(n-1))/sqrt(n)}'; done
```

`targetonly` and `binderonly` have a single chain, so they have no `iptm`; read
`ptm` and `complex_plddt` for those instead.

### What each outcome would mean

- **`alaif` and `random40` do not drop clearly below `wt`.** The metric is inert
  on designs of this quality. The mutation audit cannot run on it, and the
  finding becomes a statement about the filter rather than about W157.
- **They do drop, and `w157a` does not.** That is the project's result: the
  co-folding filter responds to the binder's chemistry but not to the target's,
  so no stage of the pipeline sees a target point mutation that leaves the
  backbone alone.
- **`w157a` drops but `e93a` and `k120a` drop equally.** The model is sensitive
  to *any* perturbation of the target sequence, not to this hotspot. That is a
  different and weaker claim, and the controls are what distinguish it.
- **`binderonly` scores poorly while `wt` scores well.** The binder does not
  fold on its own and is being held together by the target, which is the worry
  left open at stage 2 and would matter for ever making it.
